Three groups.
One platform.
Eight rooms.

La Taquería and its commissary. La Mezcalería, Chupito and the Stanley Park patio. And now Nonna’s Cucina, a block up Commercial Drive. Three companies, three sets of books, three partners — and no single place to look at any of it.

This is a bespoke build: one platform, three divisions, each with its own branding, its own data and its own budgets — and a group view for whoever is entitled to see across them. One build cost, one monthly cost, split three ways and invoiced separately so each company deducts its own. Payment plans available on the build.

01 — Before we pitched, we looked

What we found, without asking you for anything

Everything below was read from public sources in the first week of August 2026 — your own sites and menus, your ordering and booking links, delivery listings, review profiles, business-licence records and job postings. We did this before writing the proposal, because we would rather show you we understand the business than say that we do.

Most of it you probably know already. A few may be news. None of it is a criticism — it is what six rooms opened one at a time over thirteen years looks like from the outside, and most of it is fixable in weeks.

The booking path is hard to find

lamezcaleria.ca/reservations returns a 404, and the contact page has no booking link, so the only route to Tock is from the homepage. A separate OpenTable listing from 2014 tells anyone searching there that you are not bookable — worth reclaiming or retiring.

Two POS platforms, no shared view

The La Taquería venues run TouchBistro (order.tbdine.com, stores 20317, 27144, 42736). La Mezcalería’s gift cards run on Toast. Both are good systems. They just do not talk to each other, so there is no group number.

Two venues take bookings by email

Pinche on Seymour is two storeys with two patios; Comedor is 2,000 square feet with a bar. Parties of six or more at either email an inbox. Only Brentwood and Chupito are on Resy — those are covers you could be capturing.

Delivery pricing has drifted, item by item

At La Mezcalería, guacamole runs $14 to $19.50 on the apps (+39%) and an asada taco $8 to $12.75 (+59%) — but the burrito only moves $21 to $22.70 (+8%). At Comedor, tacos are up 30%, sopa 44%, churros unchanged. Set at different times by different people, and nothing currently checks any of it against commission.

There is no agave flight on the mezcalería list

La Taquería sells three flights at $25–$27. The room with roughly two hundred agave labels sells none — the only flight there is a $34 margarita flight. That looks like the easiest revenue in the group.

Happy hour runs on two different clocks

La Taquería at Brentwood runs happy hour seven days plus a late-night window to close. La Mezcalería runs two hours, weekdays only — no weekend, no late night, despite carrying the deepest bar you own.

Five companies, and the commissary moves between them

Public licence records list Yukon Food Specialties Inc, Vancouver Taco Shop Ltd, La Taqueria Taco Shop Ltd, Mexicano Taco Ltd and 1265906 BC Ltd. The Yukon tortillería produces for La Mezcalería and the Stanley Park patio — transfers that cross a company line and are worth booking properly.

The catering page is hard to find

lataqueria.com/catering exists — taco trays, 24 hours’ notice, pickup — but nothing links to it, it is absent from your sitemap and it lists no prices. La Mezcalería’s catering is published with prices. The other three venues have a page nobody can reach.

A few quick fixes

The menu tags Enchiladas de Pollo as (V,GF), and there is no legend explaining V, GF or DF. The happy-hour page lists a 12 oz cantarito pitcher at $120. Pinche and Comedor hours are not published on the site. The two Taco Bar catering tiers carry the same name and the same published inclusions at $44 and $59. All ten-minute jobs.

Every item above is checkable in about ten minutes. We list them because they are the cheapest possible demonstration of what a system watching all six rooms at once would surface on day one — not because any of them are unusual for a group this size.

03 — You asked about Google

You do not have a Google problem. You have a structure problem.

You mentioned Google exposure felt like a weak spot, so we looked properly. The instinct is right, but the diagnosis is off in a way that is good news: you have roughly four thousand Google reviews across your open venues, averaging about 4.4. That is an asset most independents never build. The problem is what surrounds it.

Your own brand search belongs to aggregators

Searching “La Taquería Vancouver” returns DoorDash, four Tripadvisor pages and Yelp — and four of those seven point at addresses you have left: North Vancouver, Hornby, Hastings. Your own site does not appear in that set at all.

Three pages, one title tag

lataqueria.com has three indexable pages for six venues, and all three publish the identical title and description. No venue name, no neighbourhood, no city. Google has nothing to tell Burnaby from Seymour Street.

Two closed listings are claimed. The open flagship is not.

On Tripadvisor, North Vancouver (closed August 2025) is claimed, with 47 reviews. Gastown (closed) is claimed. Brentwood — the flagship — is unclaimed, with 2 reviews.

Roughly 770 reviews are stranded on closed venues

555 on Hastings, 99 on North Vancouver, 89 on Gastown, 27 on Main. Sixteen years of review equity pointing customers at doors that no longer open — including an active Facebook page for closed Gastown that outranks the live restaurant for “mezcal bar Vancouver”.

No phone number. No hours. Anywhere.

lataqueria.com publishes neither, for any venue. Aggregators copy from somewhere — with no canonical source they copy each other, which is how three sources end up listing three different opening times for La Mezcalería.

Three websites that do not link to each other

lataqueria.com, lamezcaleria.ca and chupito.ca. A Michelin-recommended bar in the same building as Comedor, invisible from both. No structured data on any of the three — no Restaurant markup, no menus, no hours, in 2026.

None of that requires an SEO retainer or a rewrite. It requires claiming six profiles properly, giving each venue a page of its own, marking the closed ones closed, and linking the three sites together. Demo 1 has a Marketing module — Google & SEO, social, ads and campaigns under one roof — that lays out the whole picture and tracks it after launch.

La Mezcalería second, because the agave cellar and the Stanley Park seasonality are the most distinctive work and deserve their own attention. Then judge it on your own numbers, not ours. Six weeks in you will have real plate costs, real labour percentages and a real commissary ledger. That is a far better basis for deciding what comes next than anything in this document.

07 — You asked about TouchBistro

Yes, we can work with it. Not through an API on day one.

You asked whether we are compatible with TouchBistro and whether we can use their integration point. Here is the straight answer, because it shapes the timeline more than anything else in this proposal.

There is no public API, and no integration partner programme

TouchBistro publishes no developer portal, no API reference and no sandbox. The programme they do call a “Partner Program” is a referral scheme, and their own page says plainly that it is not intended for product integrations. Getting API access is a commercial negotiation with them, not a signup — so we will not put a date on it.

But your data can come out today, properly

TouchBistro has a Scheduled Reports feature in its cloud back office: daily, weekly or monthly exports in CSV or Excel, emailed automatically, covering sales, menu performance and staff. Daily reports arrive about two hours after your service day closes. You already own this. You just instruct it to send to us as well.

What that means in practice

Your three TouchBistro venues report the morning after, not live. Toast — at La Mezcalería and Nonna’s — is live. So the group dashboard is same-day for one venue and next-day for three, until and unless API access is granted. We would rather tell you that now than have you discover it in week three.

On the intellectual-property question

TouchBistro’s terms prohibit automated scraping of their back office — so we will not build that, and you should be wary of anyone who offers to. The same terms explicitly contemplate a merchant authorising a third party to receive their own data, which is exactly the route above. Worth knowing: TouchBistro claims ownership of aggregated, de-identified data derived from your usage. Your own transaction records remain yours to direct. Your counsel should read your signed agreement before anything is committed.

Reassuring context: TouchBistro already permits analytics partners — Avero, MarginEdge and Restaurant365 all sit in their marketplace. We are not asking for something they categorically refuse. We would open that conversation on day one and run the export route in the meantime, so nothing waits on it.

08 — What could go wrong

Three more things we would rather say now

SkipTheDishes has no public API either

Uber Eats and DoorDash publish developer documentation. Skip routes every integration through certified middleware, so it arrives as a weekly file we import and reconcile automatically. Correct numbers, seven days behind — and that will not change.

Two venues have no POS we can see

Chupito and the Stanley Park patio show no ordering or gift-card infrastructure from outside. They may be on something we connect in an afternoon, or something that needs an import route. We scope it in week one rather than quoting blind — and neither is in the first three locations we would start with.

The entity mapping needs your bookkeeper

We can see five registrants in public licence data, but not which one trades at 1622 Commercial or 367 Seymour — and two are attached to addresses since closed. Half a day with whoever keeps your books, in week one, and this stops being a risk.

And one we want to be very clear about: review removal

You asked whether the top tier could remove bad, irrelevant or very old reviews. Part of that is real and we will build it. Part of it cannot be done by anyone, and we would rather lose the feature than put an untrue claim in front of you.

What we can genuinely do

Identify reviews that breach a platform’s own published policy — written by a current or former employee, a competitor or a supplier; incentivised with a discount or a free item; off-topic; impersonation or review-farm activity; containing a staff member’s personal details; unsubstantiated criminal allegations; obscenity used to emphasise criticism. We assemble the evidence and file through the official channel. Some come down. We also draft and manage every response, in the language the review was written in.

What nobody can do

A genuine negative review cannot be removed — not by us, not by an agency, not for any fee. Nor can an old one: no platform ages reviews out, so “removing very old reviews” is not a service that exists anywhere. Firms that promise guaranteed removals are filing false reports or fraudulent copyright claims; both reverse themselves, and since October 2024 both fall within what the FTC treats as unlawful review suppression, with Canada’s Competition Bureau taking the same line.

So the honest strategy is dilution and response: you cannot delete the one-star from 2019, but you can make it the four-hundredth review instead of the fortieth. Two operational notes that come with it — Google banned staff review quotas and “please mention my name” scripts in April 2026, and Yelp forbids asking for reviews at all and publishes a public alert badge on the profiles of businesses that try. Any review-request programme we run for you will skip Yelp entirely and offer no incentives, because the alternative is a visible penalty on your own listing.

09 — Open questions

Things we could not settle from outside

Not blockers — but each changes scope, so we would rather ask than assume.

  • Which three locations would you start with? Our suggestion is Comedor, Brentwood and La Mezcalería, but you know where the pain is.
  • Is Chupito inside the group? Press in 2021 described it as operated by the group; more recent coverage names Tara Davies and Erick Vazquez as its operators. It changes the venue count and the entity map.
  • Is the Victoria location (766 Fort St) yours? Yelp and DoorDash still list it as active, but it does not appear on lataqueria.com and no recent coverage mentions it.
  • Do you control lataqueria.ca as well as lataqueria.com? Your staff email addresses use the .ca; the website is the .com. Worth confirming before we touch anything on the search side.
  • How big is the wholesale line? We can see frozen SKUs at $7.00–$29.50 on Comedor’s delivery listings. A tortillería making nixtamal masa for four kitchens looks like a wholesale business with a restaurant attached — but you would know whether you want it to be one.
  • What are the Stanley Park concession terms? Flat rent or revenue share changes how that unit’s P&L is modelled, and whether “is next season worth signing” is a question we can answer for you.
